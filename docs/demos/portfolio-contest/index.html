A portfolio is a race

Portfolio-implied contests, running in your browser.

Give every asset a runner. In each race every runner draws a performance, and the lowest draw wins. An asset's weight is the share of races it wins.

Read backwards, any long-only portfolio implies such a race: there is exactly one set of abilities that makes the runners win in proportion to the weights. Once you have the abilities you can hold them fixed, change how the runners move together, and run the race again. The new win shares are the new portfolio.

Each lane is an asset. The curve is where its draws fall, and its position is the asset's ability: further left wins more often.

A line sweeps in from the left and stops at the first dot it meets. That runner wins the race. After a few races a black tick on each bar shows the share of wins so far. It settles on the weight.

Things to try

  1. Leave the view empty. The filled bars sit exactly on the benchmark outlines. With no change in dependence, the construction returns the portfolio you gave it.
  2. Raise the everyday correlation. The three banks' dots begin to arrive together, so they compete for the same wins. Their combined weight falls and the others pick it up. Near a correlation of one the banks are one runner: the pack holds what its strongest member would win on its own, and that member keeps most of it.
  3. Set the correlation back to zero and raise the crash-together slider. Most races look independent. In the rest the cluster lands on a single extreme draw, in the tail that decides the race. The diamonds show a Gaussian race with the same correlation between the cluster's members. It moves less, because a correlation does not say where the co-movement happens.
  4. Drag a benchmark bar with a view in place. The curves slide to new abilities and the same view is applied to the new portfolio.

What is computed

The abilities come from a deterministic inverse of the independent race, by one-dimensional quadrature. The weights under the view come from 60,000 simulated races on fixed seeds, as in Algorithm 1 of the paper. The bar shows the benchmark plus the difference between the simulated race under the view and the simulated race without it, so an empty view returns the benchmark exactly.

Every asset's own distribution is a standard normal around its ability, with or without the view. The view changes only the dependence between cluster members: a Gaussian copula with the chosen correlation, and, on the chosen share of races, one shared draw in the lower tail. The animated races are fresh draws from the same law.

The paper works at a different scale: the inverse there handles factor, group and tree dependence in the reference race and hundreds of thousands of assets. The eight assets here are made up. Paper: Portfolio-Implied Contests, with an Application to Tail Risk.
