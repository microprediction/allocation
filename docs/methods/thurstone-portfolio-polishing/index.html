Methods

Thurstone portfolio polishing

Adjust a long-only portfolio for a view about how its assets move together.

A long-only portfolio can be read as the winning probabilities of a race among its assets. Polishing calibrates that race to the portfolio, changes the dependence between the assets, and runs the race again. The view may be about joint crashes, which a covariance matrix cannot express.

The method is set out in Portfolio-Implied Contests, with an Application to Tail Risk, where it is called the dependence tilt.

The race

Each asset i has an ability ai and, in each race, a noisy performance

Xi = ai + εi

The lowest performance wins. An asset's weight is the probability that it wins:

wi = P( Xi = mink Xk )

The noise ε is drawn from a joint law S, which may be correlated across assets. A Gaussian law is the usual case. Any law that can be simulated is allowed, including a copula with tail dependence.

Calibrate, change the dependence, race again

Calibrate. Choose a reference law S0 and find the abilities whose race under it reproduces the benchmark weights. The inverse is deterministic and its cost is linear in the number of assets (Cotton 2021; Cotton 2026). The reference may be independent, or correlated through a factor, group, nested or tree structure.

Change the dependence. Hold the abilities and replace the reference with a target law S1. The target carries the view: an estimated correlation, or a law in which some assets crash together.

Race again. Simulate the race under the target law. Each asset's share of the wins is its polished weight.

Writing WS for the map from abilities to weights under law S, the polished portfolio is

w = WS1( WS0−1( wbench ) )

In the Gaussian case the two laws are two correlation matrices, and a scalar φ in [0, 1] sets the strength of the view:

Ctilt = (1 − φ) Ccalib + φ Ĉ

At φ = 0 the benchmark is returned. At φ = 1 the race uses the full estimate Ĉ.

Properties

Each is stated and proved in the paper. The calibration and smoothness results are for Gaussian laws.

The benchmark is a fixed point
With no change of view the method returns the portfolio it was given.
No covariance is inverted and no program is solved
The target correlation is only sampled from, so it may be ill-conditioned or singular. The reference correlation must be positive definite.
Duplicates share one position
Replace an asset by two perfect duplicates and they split its weight equally. Every other weight is unchanged.
More dependence within a group lowers its weight
In a Gaussian race, raising a covariance between two members of a group weakly lowers the group's combined weight and weakly raises every weight outside it.
Tail consistency
For a group of equal-ability assets independent of the rest, a change in the group's joint law that makes its best performance stochastically worse does not raise the group's combined weight. In the limit where the group moves as one, it holds the weight of a single asset.
Implied objective
The weights solve a regularized program on the simplex,

w = arg maxp ∈ Δ { ⟨θ, p⟩ − ΩC(p) }

where θ = −a and ΩC is the convex conjugate of GC(θ) = E[ maxi(θi + ηi) ].
Smoothness in the correlation
If two correlation matrices have smallest eigenvalue at least λ, then

‖w(C1) − w(C0)‖1 ≤ ‖C1 − C0‖F / (√2 λ)

whatever the abilities. This bounds turnover by the size of the change of view.

Turnover on the Dow

The paper trades each method forward daily over the 28 Dow constituents with full 2010–2024 history. Every method sees the same exponentially weighted covariance. The tilt anchors to equal weight under a one-factor market reference with φ = 1.

Turnover is the mean one-way fraction of the book traded per day. Net Sharpe charges 10 basis points on each side of every trade.

The tilt turns over four to nine times less than hierarchical risk parity, minimum variance and Schur. Equal weight, inverse variance and risk parity turn over less. The tilt's gross Sharpe is below every method in the table except minimum variance.

Limits

  • The weights are winning probabilities, so the portfolio is long-only and fully invested.
  • Benchmark weights must be strictly positive. Zero weights are dropped or floored before calibration.
  • The race sees only relative performance. A shock common to every asset leaves the weights unchanged.
  • The paper does not test whether a tail view improves drawdown or expected shortfall out of sample.
  • The choice of reference law and of φ, and the treatment of estimation error in the abilities, are open.

Use

from allocation import ThurstonePortfolio

est = ThurstonePortfolio(calib="market", phi=1.0)
est.fit(returns)            # returns: (n_obs, n_assets)
est.weights_                # long-only weights that sum to one

for batch in stream:        # rebalancing
    est.partial_fit(batch)  # same random seeds, new correlation
    w = est.weights_

partial_fit keeps the simulation's random seeds fixed and re-colours the same paths with the new correlation. The weights then move with the correlation and not with sampling noise.

Expressing a tail view

Two built-in routes. A Student-t race gives every pair of assets tail dependence. A downside covariance makes the race respond to how assets move together when they fall.

from allocation import ThurstonePortfolio, DownsideSemicovariance

ThurstonePortfolio(sampler="student_t", nu=4).fit(returns).weights_
ThurstonePortfolio(covariance_estimator=DownsideSemicovariance()).fit(returns).weights_

The three steps as functions

import numpy as np
from allocation import abilities_from_weights, weights_from_abilities, tilt_weights

w = np.array([0.4, 0.25, 0.2, 0.15])
a = abilities_from_weights(w)      # calibrate: weights to abilities
weights_from_abilities(a)          # race: returns w to within 1e-8

tilt_weights(benchmark, cov, phi=0.5)   # all three steps on any long-only benchmark
