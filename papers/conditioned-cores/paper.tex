\begin{abstract}
Hierarchical core-orbital allocation \parencite{hcoa2026} optimizes across
one representative asset per cluster and then redistributes each cluster's
budget among the remaining members. The method is presented as an
architecture, with its properties stated rather than derived from an
objective. We observe that the minimum-variance portfolio already has a
core-orbital form: block inversion writes it as one problem per cluster on
a Schur-complemented block, and under the assumption that orbitals depend on
other clusters only through their own core, that complement is taken
against the other cores alone. Feeding those conditioned blocks, damped by
$\gamma\in[0,1]$, to the published architecture gives a family whose
$\gamma=0$ end is the published method and whose $\gamma=1$ end is the
global optimum, at the same cost. The same holds for every strategic
objective that is minimum variance in disguise, maximum Sharpe included.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\textcite{hcoa2026} proposes hierarchical core-orbital allocation, HCOA, for
universes with strong systematic structure such as USD-denominated bonds.
Assets are clustered. Within each cluster one representative ``core'' asset
is chosen: the medoid, the member most aligned with the cluster's first
principal component, or the member with the largest sum of squared
correlations to the rest. A strategic optimizer, with any objective,
allocates across the cores. A tactical rule, ``hierarchical rules or local
optimization'', then redistributes each cluster's budget among its
``orbital'' members, and the cluster totals reproduce the strategic
decision.

The paper claims three properties: numerical robustness, because the
strategic problem has one dimension per cluster; functional flexibility,
because the two tiers can use different objectives; and structural
coherence, because the tiers agree on cluster totals. It places itself in
the tradition of hierarchical risk parity \parencite{lopezdeprado2016} and
nested clustered optimization \parencite{lopezdeprado2020}, and its stated
objection to HRP and HERC is that they ``implement an allocation logic that
cannot be modified without altering the entire algorithm''. On a USD bond
universe rebalanced quarterly from 2014 to 2025 its risk-adjusted
performance is statistically comparable to NCO, which the paper names as
its closest two-stage relative, with more diversification.

The three properties are stated from the architecture rather than derived
from an objective. That leaves a natural question open. Coherence holds for
any top-down scheme, flexibility says what the tiers may do but not what
they should do, and the robustness comes from working in fewer dimensions
without a statement of what the reduction approximates. The method has no
limiting object and no parameter that moves toward one.

Schur-complementary allocation \parencite{cotton2024schur} answers the
same question for the bisection tree. It starts from the minimum-variance
portfolio, observes that the weights on
any block are governed by that block's Schur complement, and defines a
recursion that relaxes the global optimum by a coupling $\gamma\in[0,1]$.
Every step is a consequence of one identity. The hierarchy, the augmented
blocks, and the budget split are not design choices.

This note makes one observation and develops its consequence: the
minimum-variance portfolio is already core-orbital. The architecture
survives intact. What changes is that each block HCOA reads
is replaced by a Schur-complemented block, and the cores acquire a precise
role: they are the variables one conditions on, not stand-ins for their
clusters. The result is exact at $\gamma=1$ under an assumption the
clustering already makes, and costs what HCOA costs.

\section{The recipe as published}

Let $\Sigma\succ0$ be the covariance of $n$ assets partitioned into
clusters $I_1,\dots,I_k$. Cluster $i$ has a core $c_i\in I_i$ and orbitals
$O_i=I_i\setminus\{c_i\}$. Write $C=\{c_1,\dots,c_k\}$ for the set of
cores and $C_{-i}=C\setminus\{c_i\}$. For index sets $X,Y$ write
$\Sigma_{XY}$ for the corresponding block, $\Sigma_{ii}$ for the block of
cluster $i$, and $-i$ for the complement of $I_i$.

HCOA reads two kinds of block.\footnote{We work from the abstract, the
author's announcement, \url{https://lnkd.in/p/ekyaNa-E}, and indexed
excerpts of the full text. The tactical rule is described there as
``hierarchical rules or local optimization'' within the cluster, which we
read as a rule on the cluster's own block. If a published variant consults
other clusters, the $\gamma=0$ corner below moves but the family does
not.} The strategic tier solves a
$k$-dimensional problem on the core covariance,
\begin{equation}
  a \;=\; \argminop_{a\in\R^k:\;\ones^\top a=1} f\!\left(a;\,\Sigma_{CC}\right),
  \label{eq:hcoa-top}
\end{equation}
for an objective $f$ such as variance, negative Sharpe ratio, or a
risk-parity criterion. The tactical tier applies a rule $g$ to the cluster's
own block, $v_i = g(\Sigma_{ii})$ with $\ones^\top v_i=1$, and assigns
$w_{I_i}=a_i v_i$. Cluster $i$ receives exactly $a_i$, which is the
coherence property.

Both blocks are marginal. The strategic tier sees the cores' covariance
with each other and nothing about the orbitals. The tactical tier sees one
cluster and nothing outside it. The only channel by which cluster $i$'s
weights respond to cluster $j$ is the scalar $a_i$.

\section{The global optimum in core-orbital coordinates}

Fix a cluster $i$ and partition the assets into $I_i$ and its complement.
Block inversion of $\Sigma$ gives, for the minimum-variance direction
$\Sigma^{-1}\ones$, the standard identity
\parencite[App.~A]{cotton2024schur}
\begin{equation}
  \big(\Sigma^{-1}\ones\big)_{I_i}
  \;=\; \big(\Sigma^{c}_{ii}\big)^{-1} b_i,
  \label{eq:blockinv}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma^{c}_{ii} = \Sigma_{ii}-\Sigma_{i,-i}\Sigma_{-i,-i}^{-1}\Sigma_{-i,i},
  \qquad
  b_i = \ones-\Sigma_{i,-i}\Sigma_{-i,-i}^{-1}\ones .
  \label{eq:complement}
\end{equation}
The minimum-variance weights on cluster $i$ are the solution of a problem
posed on cluster $i$ alone, once its block has been replaced by the
covariance of cluster $i$ conditional on everything else, and the
constraint vector $\ones$ has been replaced by the companion $b_i$.

There is no cross-cluster optimizer in the global optimum. It is $k$
within-cluster problems stacked, and the cluster totals are whatever those
problems return. Every piece of cross-cluster information enters through
the conditioning, not through a top-level objective.

HCOA routes the information the other way. All cross-cluster information
enters through the $k\times k$ objective on marginal blocks, and none
reaches the within-cluster step.

The conditioning in \eqref{eq:complement} is against all $n-|I_i|$ other
assets. That is exact and expensive. Under the orbital model below it
collapses to conditioning on $k-1$ cores.

\section{The orbital model}

The announcement of HCOA says its ``cores capture systematic factors
while orbitals preserve instrument-level diversification''. The \emph{orbital model} is that
sentence as a second-moment restriction.

\begin{definition}[Orbital model]
For every cluster $j$, the residual of the orbitals $O_j$ after linear
regression on their own core $c_j$ is uncorrelated with every asset outside
$I_j$. Equivalently $\Sigma_{O_j,-j}=\Sigma_{O_j c_j}\,\sigma_{c_j}^{-2}\,\Sigma_{c_j,-j}$.
\end{definition}

An orbital may be correlated with anything, but its correlation with other
clusters passes entirely through its core. This is a rank-one restriction
on each cross-cluster block, and it is what makes a core a summary of its
cluster rather than a member of it. HCOA's own core selection, the member
most aligned with the cluster's first principal component or with the
largest summed squared correlation to the rest, is a search for the asset
that makes this restriction least false.

\begin{proposition}[Cores are sufficient]
\label{prop:sufficient}
Under the orbital model, for each cluster $i$,
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma^{c}_{ii}
  \;=\; \Sigma_{ii}-\Sigma_{i,C_{-i}}\,\Sigma_{C_{-i}C_{-i}}^{-1}\,\Sigma_{C_{-i},i},
  \qquad
  b_i \;=\; \ones-\Sigma_{i,C_{-i}}\,\Sigma_{C_{-i}C_{-i}}^{-1}\,\ones .
  \label{eq:cheap}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The matrix $\Sigma_{i,-i}\Sigma_{-i,-i}^{-1}$ holds the coefficients of the
linear regression of cluster $i$ on all other assets. Split the regressors
into the other cores $C_{-i}$ and the other orbitals $O_{-i}$, and write
$O_{-i}=\beta\,C_{-i}+\varepsilon$ for the block-diagonal regression of
each orbital set on its own core. The map from $(C_{-i},O_{-i})$ to
$(C_{-i},\varepsilon)$ is invertible, so the regression may be run on the
latter pair. Under the orbital model $\varepsilon$ is uncorrelated with
cluster $i$ and with $C_{-i}$, so its coefficients vanish and the
coefficients on $C_{-i}$ are $\Sigma_{i,C_{-i}}\Sigma_{C_{-i}C_{-i}}^{-1}$.
Substituting into \eqref{eq:blockinv} gives \eqref{eq:cheap}, the $\ones$
in $b_i$ contributing only through its $C_{-i}$ entries.
\end{proof}

Conditioning cluster $i$ on the other cores is the same as conditioning it
on everything. The inverse in \eqref{eq:cheap} is $(k-1)\times(k-1)$.

\section{Conditioned cores}

Following \textcite{cotton2024schur}, damp the conditioning. For
$\gamma\in[0,1]$ define the \emph{conditioned pair} of cluster $i$,
\begin{equation}
  Q_i(\gamma) \;=\; \Sigma_{ii}-\gamma\,\Sigma_{i,C_{-i}}\Sigma_{C_{-i}C_{-i}}^{-1}\Sigma_{C_{-i},i},
  \qquad
  b_i(\gamma) \;=\; \ones-\gamma\,\Sigma_{i,C_{-i}}\Sigma_{C_{-i}C_{-i}}^{-1}\ones .
  \label{eq:pair}
\end{equation}
At $\gamma=0$ the pair is the marginal block and the ones vector, which is
what HCOA reads. At $\gamma=1$ it is the conditioned block of
\cref{prop:sufficient}.

The recipe keeps HCOA's two tiers and changes what they are handed.

\paragraph{Tactical tier.}
Within cluster $i$, apply a rule to the pair $(Q_i(\gamma),b_i(\gamma))$
to obtain $v_i$ with $\ones^\top v_i=1$. The canonical rule is constrained
minimum variance, $v_i\propto Q_i(\gamma)^{-1}b_i(\gamma)$. A rule that
reads only a covariance can be given the collapsed matrix
$\big(Q_i^{-1}\circ b_ib_i^\top\big)^{-1}$ of \textcite{cotton2024schur}
when $b_i$ has no zero entry.

\paragraph{Strategic tier.}
Let $V$ be the $n\times k$ matrix with $v_i$ in column $i$, supported on
$I_i$. Allocate across clusters with any objective on the induced
$k\times k$ covariance $V^\top\Sigma V$,
\begin{equation}
  a \;=\; \argminop_{a:\;\ones^\top a=1} f\!\left(a;\,V^\top\Sigma V\right),
  \qquad w = Va .
  \label{eq:top}
\end{equation}
Cluster $i$ receives $a_i$, so coherence holds as before. The summary asset
for cluster $i$ is no longer the core but the conditioned within-cluster
portfolio $v_i$.

\begin{proposition}[The $\gamma=1$ end is minimum variance]
\label{prop:exact}\footnote{\texttt{verify\_conditioned\_cores.py} next to
the manuscript checks \cref{prop:sufficient}, this proposition, and
\cref{prop:disguise} with a random exposure vector, on random covariances
satisfying the orbital model, to $10^{-10}$, and confirms that all fail
when the model is violated.}
Under the orbital model, with $\gamma=1$ and minimum variance at both
tiers, $w$ in \eqref{eq:top} is the global minimum-variance portfolio.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:blockinv} and \cref{prop:sufficient}, the block of
$w^\star\propto\Sigma^{-1}\ones$ on cluster $i$ is proportional to
$Q_i(1)^{-1}b_i(1)$, hence to $v_i$. So $w^\star$ lies in the column span
of $V$ and sums to one. The strategic problem minimizes variance over
exactly that span with the same constraint, and $w^\star$ minimizes
variance over all fully-invested portfolios, so the two minimizers
coincide.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Cores as conditioning variables]
At $\gamma=0$ the cores play no role in the tactical tier, and with the
selection rule they play the only role in the strategic tier. At
$\gamma>0$ the cores enter every cluster's block as regressors. A core
``captures systematic factors'' by being the variable the other clusters
are conditioned on. The selection rule $v_i=e_{c_i}$ does not read $Q_i$
or $b_i$. It is closest to the conditioned rule when the cluster is nearly
a single asset, which is the regime the core-selection criteria of
\textcite{hcoa2026} aim for.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Minimum variance in disguise]
\label{prop:disguise}
Let the strategic objective have global solution $w^\star\propto\Sigma^{-1}u$
for some vector $u$, and replace $\ones$ by $u$ in \eqref{eq:pair},
\begin{equation}
  b_i(\gamma) \;=\; u_{I_i}-\gamma\,\Sigma_{i,C_{-i}}\Sigma_{C_{-i}C_{-i}}^{-1}u_{C_{-i}} .
  \label{eq:pair-u}
\end{equation}
Then \cref{prop:sufficient} and \cref{prop:exact} hold verbatim: under the
orbital model, with $\gamma=1$, the tactical rule $v_i\propto Q_i^{-1}b_i$
and the same objective at the strategic tier return $w^\star$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The block identity \eqref{eq:blockinv} is the top block of $\Sigma^{-1}u$
for any $u$, with $\ones$ replaced by $u$ throughout, and the proof of
\cref{prop:sufficient} never used that the vector was $\ones$. So the block
of $w^\star$ on cluster $i$ is proportional to $Q_i(1)^{-1}b_i(1)$, hence to
$v_i$, and $w^\star$ lies in the column span of $V$. The strategic problem
optimizes the same objective over that span, so it returns $w^\star$.
\end{proof}

This covers maximum Sharpe ratio, $u=\mu$; mean--variance at any risk
aversion, $u$ a combination of $\mu$ and $\ones$; and minimum variance under
a prescribed exposure, $u$ the exposure vector. Each is minimum variance
with a different constraint vector, and the companion $b_i$ is how the
pair form carries that vector into the cluster. Risk parity is not of this
form. The recipe still runs, since $Q_i(\gamma)$ is a covariance whatever
$f$ is, but nothing is exact at $\gamma=1$.

\begin{remark}[The strategic objective]
The objective $f$ in \eqref{eq:top} is arbitrary, so the strategic
freedom HCOA advertises is retained, and by \cref{prop:disguise} the
exactness at $\gamma=1$ is retained for the quadratic ones. With minimum
variance and $\gamma=1$ the optimizer is redundant. The ratio rule of
\textcite{cotton2024schur}, $a_i \propto \ones^\top Q_i^{-1} b_i$, returns
the same $a$ without solving \eqref{eq:top}. For other objectives the
$k\times k$ problem is where they live, and it now reads a covariance of
conditioned cluster portfolios rather than of single bonds.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What $\gamma$ means]
The cross-core coefficients $\Sigma_{i,C_{-i}}\Sigma_{C_{-i}C_{-i}}^{-1}$
are estimated. \textcite{cotton2026bridge} show that with estimation error
the out-of-sample-optimal $\gamma$ generically lies strictly inside
$(0,1)$, and the argument transfers unchanged, since the cross-core block
is the only object $\gamma$ touches. So $\gamma$ is how much of the
estimated cross-core coupling to trust, and the interior is where one
expects to sit.
\end{remark}

\section{Cost}

HCOA inverts, or otherwise optimizes over, one $k\times k$ matrix and then
solves $k$ problems of size $|I_i|$. The conditioned recipe adds the
regression coefficients in \eqref{eq:pair}. These need $\Sigma_{CC}^{-1}$
once, with a rank-one downdate per cluster to remove $c_i$, followed by a
product with a $|I_i|\times(k-1)$ block. The count of large inverses is
unchanged and the dimension of the strategic tier is unchanged. The
robustness HCOA obtains from dimension is therefore kept, with a reason
attached: under the orbital model the $k-1$ cores are all the conditioning
there is.

If the orbital model fails, \eqref{eq:cheap} is an approximation to the
full complement in \eqref{eq:blockinv}, and the recipe is a Schur allocation
with a truncated conditioning set. The model is directly testable:
regress each orbital on its core and on the other cores, and check that the
other-core coefficients vanish.

\section{Closing}

The contribution is deliberately small. The architecture belongs to
\textcite{hcoa2026}, the identity is block inversion, and what is added is
the pairing of the two and the dial.

The core-orbital architecture is sound, and \textcite{hcoa2026} motivates
it by the properties it has. Block inversion supplies the object it
approximates. The minimum-variance portfolio is
already core-orbital, and the orbital model, which is the clustering
premise stated as a second-moment restriction, makes it cheap. Replacing
HCOA's marginal blocks with the damped conditioned pairs
\eqref{eq:pair} gives a one-parameter family with the published method at
$\gamma=0$, the global optimum at $\gamma=1$, and a documented reason to sit
between them.

We leave to future work an empirical comparison on the USD bond universe
of \textcite{hcoa2026}, where the orbital model can be tested by the
regression above before any portfolio is built.
