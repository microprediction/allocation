\begin{abstract}
The Schur-complementary allocation of \textcite{cotton2024schur} interpolates
hierarchical risk parity (HRP, $\gamma = 0$) and minimum variance
($\gamma \to 1$) through a coupling parameter $\gamma$ that scales how much of
the cross-block covariance the recursion uses. \textcite{antonov2025hrp} prove
analytically that of the two endpoints, HRP is the less noisy under covariance
estimation error; \textcite{mograby2025} shows that on hierarchical graph
structures the $\gamma = 1$ endpoint is computable exactly by the same
recursion. This note proves that, generically, \emph{neither endpoint is
optimal}. First, a structural lemma: the $\gamma = 0$ recursion never reads
the top-level cross-block, so cross-block noise cannot move it. Second, if
coupling has first-order population value, then for all sufficiently small
noise the expected out-of-sample variance has $F'(0) < 0$: HRP is strictly
suboptimal. Third, because every appearance of the estimated cross-block in
the recursion carries a factor of $\gamma$, the noise cost is $O(\gamma^2)$
near zero while it is order one at $\gamma = 1$; above an explicit noise
threshold the minimizer is interior. A four-asset witness makes the
conclusion unconditional: with a two-point error on one cross-block entry,
$F(9/10) < F(0)$ and $F(9/10) < F(1)$ hold as \emph{exact rational
inequalities}, machine-verifiable because the recursion is rational
arithmetic.
\end{abstract}

\section{The recursion and the two endpoints}
\label{sec:setup}

Fix an asset order and a balanced bisection tree over $n$ assets. Given a
symmetric positive-definite matrix $M$ and coupling $\gamma \in [0,1]$, the
Schur-complementary allocation $w(\gamma; M)$ is defined recursively
\parencite{cotton2024schur}: at a node with children $(L, R)$ and blocks
\[
  M = \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ B^\top & D \end{pmatrix},
\]
each child block is \emph{augmented} with the $\gamma$-scaled Schur complement
of the other,
\begin{equation}
  A_\gamma \;=\; \operatorname{sym}\!\Big[\big(I - \gamma B D^{-1}\big)^{-1}
  \big(A - \gamma\, B D^{-1} B^\top\big)\Big],
  \qquad
  D_\gamma \;=\; (\text{same with } A \leftrightarrow D,\ B \to B^\top),
  \label{eq:aug}
\end{equation}
the budget is split between children in inverse proportion to the naive
portfolio variances $\nu(A_\gamma), \nu(D_\gamma)$ (inverse-diagonal weights),
and the recursion continues inside each augmented block. At $\gamma = 0$ the
augmentation is the identity and the recursion is HRP over the fixed order; as
$\gamma \to 1$ it recovers minimum variance
\parencite{cotton2024schur,lopezdeprado2016}, exactly so on the hierarchical
structures of \textcite{mograby2025}.

Let $\Sigma \succ 0$ be the population covariance and $\widehat\Sigma_\tau$ an
estimate, $\widehat\Sigma_\tau = \Sigma + \tau N$ with $N$ a random symmetric
perturbation, bounded, defined on a set where all matrices appearing in
\eqref{eq:aug} remain invertible for $\gamma \in [0, \bar\gamma]$. The object
of study is the expected out-of-sample variance
\begin{equation}
  F(\gamma; \tau) \;=\; \E\big[\, w(\gamma; \widehat\Sigma_\tau)^\top\, \Sigma\,
  w(\gamma; \widehat\Sigma_\tau) \,\big],
  \qquad
  V_0(\gamma) \;=\; F(\gamma; 0),
  \label{eq:F}
\end{equation}
the population curve. Every operation in the recursion is rational in the
entries of $(\gamma, M)$, so $w$, $F$, and $V_0$ are rational functions on the
domain where denominators do not vanish; in particular they are $C^\infty$
there, which is all the regularity used below.

\section{Three results}

\begin{lemma}[The HRP end is blind to the cross-block]
\label{lem:blind}
$w(0; M)$ does not depend on the top-level cross-block $B$: it is a function
of $(A, D)$ alone. Consequently, if the estimation error is supported on the
top-level cross-block ($N_{LL} = 0$, $N_{RR} = 0$), then $w(0;
\widehat\Sigma_\tau) = w(0; \Sigma)$ almost surely and $F(0; \tau) = V_0(0)$
exactly, for every $\tau$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At $\gamma = 0$ the augmentation \eqref{eq:aug} returns its first argument, so
the top-level split uses $\nu(A)$ and $\nu(D)$, which read only within-block
entries, and the recursion continues inside the \emph{unmodified} blocks $A$
and $D$, never touching $B$. (Deeper cross-blocks --- those interior to $A$ or
$D$ --- are read through the naive variances of their parents; only the
top-level $B$ is never read.)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[HRP is strictly suboptimal under small noise]
\label{thm:nohrp}
Suppose the population curve strictly decreases at the HRP end,
$V_0'(0) < 0$ --- coupling has first-order population value. Then there is
$\tau_0 > 0$ such that for all $\tau < \tau_0$,
\[
  \frac{\partial F}{\partial\gamma}(0; \tau) \;<\; 0,
\]
so every minimizer of $F(\cdot\,; \tau)$ on $[0, \bar\gamma]$ is bounded away
from $0$. No support restriction on $N$ is needed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\partial_\gamma F(0;\tau) = \E[\partial_\gamma V(0; \Sigma + \tau N)]$ where
$V(\gamma; M) = w(\gamma; M)^\top \Sigma\, w(\gamma; M)$ is $C^1$ in a
neighborhood of $(0, \Sigma)$ with $\partial_\gamma V$ continuous and, for
bounded $N$, dominated on that neighborhood. By dominated convergence
$\partial_\gamma F(0;\tau) \to \partial_\gamma V(0;\Sigma) = V_0'(0) < 0$ as
$\tau \to 0$; continuity in $\tau$ gives the threshold $\tau_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full coupling is suboptimal above a noise threshold]
\label{thm:nofull}
Let the error be supported on the top-level cross-block and symmetric
($N \overset{d}{=} -N$). Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] \emph{(Quadratic noise-cost at the HRP end.)} There is a
constant $C < \infty$, uniform on a compact set of $\gamma$ and noise
realizations keeping \eqref{eq:aug} invertible, with
\[
  \big|\, F(\gamma; \tau) - V_0(\gamma) \,\big| \;\le\; C\, \gamma^2 \tau^2 .
\]
\item[\textup{(ii)}] If for some $\hat\gamma \in (0, 1)$ and $\tau^*$ the
excess noise cost at full coupling satisfies
\[
  F(1; \tau^*) - V_0(1) \;>\; V_0(\hat\gamma) - V_0(1) + C \hat\gamma^2
  \tau^{*2},
\]
then $F(\hat\gamma; \tau^*) < F(1; \tau^*)$, and together with
\Cref{thm:nohrp} every minimizer of $F(\cdot\,; \tau^*)$ is interior.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) In \eqref{eq:aug} the cross-block enters only through the products
$\gamma \widehat B D^{-1}$ and $\gamma \widehat B D^{-1} \widehat B^\top$, so
every occurrence of the perturbation $\tau N_B$ in the recursion carries the
prefactor $\gamma$; the deeper levels operate on the augmented blocks, whose
$N_B$-dependence retains that prefactor. Hence $\partial w / \partial\tau =
\gamma\, h(\gamma, \tau)$ with $h$ bounded on the stated compact set, so
$V(\gamma; \tau N) - V(\gamma; 0)$ is Lipschitz in $\tau$ with constant
$O(\gamma)$, and by symmetry of $N$ the first-order term in $\tau$ cancels in
expectation, leaving $|F - V_0| \le C\gamma^2\tau^2$ by Taylor's theorem with
the (uniformly bounded) second derivative. (ii) is arithmetic:
$F(\hat\gamma;\tau^*) \le V_0(\hat\gamma) + C\hat\gamma^2\tau^{*2} <
F(1;\tau^*)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The hypothesis of \Cref{thm:nofull}(ii) is mild in practice and easily
checked: the left side is the realized noise cost of trusting the estimated
cross-block fully, which grows without bound as the perturbed recursion
approaches the boundary of the positive-definite cone --- beyond it, the
$\gamma = 1$ recursion is not even defined and any repair produces an $O(1)$
jump, the catastrophic version of the same phenomenon. The right side is a
population bias gap plus an $O(\hat\gamma^2\tau^2)$ term that the numerical
sections below find to be orders of magnitude smaller (the measured constant
is $C \approx 5\times10^{-4}$ in the witness of \Cref{prop:witness}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The sign of $F - V_0$ is \emph{not} fixed: symmetric noise can reduce expected
out-of-sample variance slightly at moderate $\gamma$ through the curvature of
$V$ in the perturbation (observed numerically in the witness below). The
theorems accordingly bound $|F - V_0|$ and never assume noise is costly
pointwise; what they use is only that it costs nothing at $\gamma = 0$
(\Cref{lem:blind}) and that its magnitude at $\gamma = 1$ can exceed the
population gain of full coupling.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Exact witness]
\label{prop:witness}
Let $n = 4$ with blocks $\{1,2\}, \{3,4\}$, volatilities $(1,1,2,2)$,
within-block correlation $\tfrac12$, cross-block correlation $\tfrac{3}{10}$;
let the estimate err in the $(1,3)$ covariance entry by $\pm\tfrac{3}{20}
\cdot 2$ with probability $\tfrac12$ each. Then, in exact rational arithmetic,
\[
  F(\tfrac{9}{10}) < F(0) \quad\text{(margin } 4.879\times10^{-2}\text{)},
  \qquad
  F(\tfrac{9}{10}) < F(1) \quad\text{(margin } 5.702\times10^{-4}\text{)},
\]
so every minimizer of $F$ on $[0,1]$ is strictly interior. All augmented
blocks remain positive definite on both noise branches for every
$\gamma \in [0,1]$: no repair or projection is involved.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$F$ at rational arguments is an exact rational number, being a finite
composition of field operations \eqref{eq:aug} on rational inputs; the two
inequalities are evaluated in exact fraction arithmetic by the accompanying
script (\texttt{experiments/schur\_interior\_optimum.py} in the
\texttt{allocation} repository), which also cross-checks the closed-form
recursion against the package implementation and certifies positive
definiteness along the path.
\end{proof}

\section{Placement}

\textcite{antonov2025hrp} compare the endpoints analytically and prove HRP is
the less noisy; \textcite{mograby2025} shows the $\gamma = 1$ endpoint is
computable exactly on hierarchical structures. The present results compose
with both: exactness of full coupling does not confer statistical optimality
(\Cref{thm:nofull}), and the noise-robustness of HRP does not confer it either
(\Cref{thm:nohrp}) --- under estimation error the optimum generically lies
strictly between, which is the empirical finding of the walk-forward bridge on
Dow data (fitted on one-year windows, evaluated out of sample, minimum near
$\gamma \approx 0.55$, robust to Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage
\parencite{ledoitwolf2004} of the training covariance). The coupling
$\gamma$ is a bias--variance dial, and the right setting is interior.
