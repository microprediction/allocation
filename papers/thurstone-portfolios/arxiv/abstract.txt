We use a recently developed contest inversion algorithm as a device for portfolio construction and augmentation, and in particular we demonstrate how an investor can inject views about tail dependence into an existing portfolio by this means. Each asset is a runner in a Thurstone race whose noisy performances may be correlated through factor or hierarchical structure, and its weight is the probability that it finishes first. A fast deterministic inverse recovers abilities from benchmark weights, and a simulation re-runs the race under new dependence. Neither inverts a covariance or solves an optimization. Given a factor model the cost is linear in the number of assets, and thousands of names fit in seconds. Near-duplicates split a position rather than double it. We prove benchmark reproduction, an implied convex objective and a turnover bound.
