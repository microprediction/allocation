\begin{abstract}
We tilt an allocation on the simplex under a view on dependence rather than
returns. Each asset draws a noisy performance, and its weight is read as the
probability that it comes out best. A fast deterministic inverse backs the
abilities out of the benchmark, and the tilt re-runs the race under a richer
dependence law. The result is a tail-sensitive analogue of Black--Litterman that
inverts no covariance and solves no optimization. A near-duplicate asset splits a
position rather than doubling it, as Luce-type rules such as capitalization
weighting do. We prove feasibility, benchmark reproduction, redundancy and tail
consistency, an implied convex objective, and a Lipschitz turnover bound. On the
Dow constituents over 2010--2024 the tilt turns over four to nine times less than
minimum variance and the clustering allocators.
\end{abstract}
