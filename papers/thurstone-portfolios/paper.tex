\begin{abstract}
We take an allocation on the simplex and tilt it under a view on dependence
rather than returns. A weight is read as a winning probability: each asset draws
a noisy performance and its weight is the probability it comes out best. A fast
deterministic inverse backs the abilities out of the benchmark, and the tilt is
the race re-run under a richer dependence law. This is a tail-sensitive analogue
of Black--Litterman. The view is on co-movement, tails included, and no
covariance is inverted and no optimization solved. A near-duplicate asset splits
a position rather than doubling it. Luce-type rules count it twice, and
capitalization weighting is one. We prove feasibility, benchmark reproduction,
redundancy and tail consistency, an implied convex objective, and a Lipschitz
turnover bound. In a known-truth lab the tail-dependent tilt de-duplicates the
names that crash together. A Dow walk-forward runs at four to nine times less
turnover than clustering allocators.
\end{abstract}
