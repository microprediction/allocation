\begin{abstract}
We introduce for the first time the use of relatively new contest inversion
algorithms as a device for portfolio construction and augmentation. Each asset is
a runner in a Thurstone race, drawing a noisy performance, and its weight is the
probability that it finishes first. A fast deterministic inverse recovers
abilities from benchmark weights, and a factor-form simulation re-runs the race
under new dependence. Neither
inverts a covariance or solves an optimization, and the cost is linear in the
number of assets, so thousands of names fit in seconds. The result is a
tail-sensitive Black--Litterman in which near-duplicates split a position rather
than double it. We prove benchmark reproduction, an implied
convex objective and a turnover bound. On the Dow constituents it turns over four to
nine times less than minimum variance.
\end{abstract}
