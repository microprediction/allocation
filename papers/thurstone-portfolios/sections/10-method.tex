\section{The race and the dependence tilt}
\label{sec:method}
\label{sec:model}

This section defines the race, the map between abilities and weights, and the
two-step construction built on it.

Consider a universe of $n$ assets. Each asset has a latent ability $a_i\in\R$ and
a noisy \emph{performance}
\begin{equation}
  X_i = a_i + \varepsilon_i, \qquad \varepsilon = (\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_n)
  \sim S,
  \label{eq:performance}
\end{equation}
where $S$ is any centered joint law, the \emph{simulation} that drives the race,
and may be correlated across assets. We adopt the convention that the
\emph{minimum} performance wins, so that a smaller ability denotes a stronger
competitor. Each asset is a \emph{competitor} in the race, and its portfolio weight
is synonymous with its \emph{winning probability},
\begin{equation}
  p_i \;=\; \Prob\!\left( X_i = \min_{k} X_k \right).
  \label{eq:stateprice}
\end{equation}

The Gaussian $S = \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_\varepsilon)$ is the workhorse, and the
only case in which the calibration and smoothness results below are closed-form.
The construction and the implementation treat $S$ as a black-box sampler, so a
copula with genuine tail dependence, a fat-tailed or skewed law, or a structured
generator are equally admissible (\Cref{lem:tail} and \Cref{rem:anysim}).

\paragraph{The population map.}
Write $\theta = -a$ and $\eta = -\varepsilon$, so a larger $\theta_i$ denotes a
stronger competitor and $\max_i(\theta_i + \eta_i) = -\min_i(a_i + \varepsilon_i)$,
and let
\begin{equation}
  G_S(\theta) = \E\Big[\max_i(\theta_i + \eta_i)\Big], \quad
  \eta = -\varepsilon,\ \ \varepsilon\sim S, \qquad
  W_S(\theta) = \nabla G_S(\theta) \in \Delta,
  \label{eq:popmap}
\end{equation}
so $W_S(\theta)$ is the vector of winning probabilities \eqref{eq:stateprice} (the
gradient identity is \Cref{thm:objective}). The reflection $\eta = -\varepsilon$ is
immaterial for a symmetric $S$. For a skewed or tail-asymmetric law it is what
keeps the maximum in \eqref{eq:popmap} reading the same tail as the minimum in
\eqref{eq:stateprice}.

The forward map $W_S$ takes abilities to weights. Its inverse recovers abilities
from weights, up to an additive constant. Both are computed deterministically, at
cost linear in $n$, by the method of \textcite{cotton2026inversion}.\footnote{An earlier version of this
technique used the original lattice method of \textcite{cotton2021}. The 2026
methodology, which uses a shared field, is preferred.} It covers independent
performances and performances correlated through a factor, block, nested or tree
covariance.

\paragraph{The dependence tilt.}
The construction uses two laws, a \emph{reference} $S_0$ and a \emph{target} $S_1$.
Calibration to a benchmark $w^{\mathrm{target}}\in\operatorname{int}\Delta$ under
the reference inverts the population map,
$\theta^0 = W_{S_0}^{-1}(w^{\mathrm{target}})$ up to an additive constant
(\Cref{lem:calib}). The \emph{dependence tilt} re-runs the race under the target at
the same abilities,
\begin{equation}
  T_{S_0\to S_1}(w^{\mathrm{target}}) \;=\; W_{S_1}\!\big(W_{S_0}^{-1}(w^{\mathrm{target}})\big).
  \label{eq:tiltmap}
\end{equation}

In the Gaussian workhorse the two laws are two correlation matrices, a reference
$C_{\mathrm{calib}}$ and the estimated return correlation $C_{\mathrm{tilt}}$, and
the tilt is the portfolio's response to $C_{\mathrm{tilt}} - C_{\mathrm{calib}}$
(\Cref{rem:fixedpoint}). The target law may carry dependence, tail dependence
included, that the reference does not.

Almost everything below is a property of \eqref{eq:tiltmap}: index reproduction is
$T_{S_0\to S_0} = \mathrm{id}$ (\Cref{rem:fixedpoint}); the implied objective is
the Fenchel dual of $G_{S_1}$ (\Cref{thm:objective}); redundancy and tail
consistency are properties of $W_{S_1}$ at the fixed $\theta^0$
(\Cref{lem:redundancy,lem:tail}); and the tilt's smoothness is that of $W_{S_1}$
(\Cref{thm:smooth}). The Gaussian instance, in which $S_0, S_1$ are two correlation
matrices, is \Cref{alg:tilt} below. \Cref{fig:square} lays the composition out as a
square.

\begin{lemma}[Calibration]
\label{lem:calib}
For a Gaussian reference $S_0 = \mathcal N(0, C_0)$ with $C_0 \succ 0$, the map
$W_{C_0} : \R^n/\!\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\} \to \operatorname{int}\Delta$ is a
smooth bijection with smooth inverse; equivalently, every strictly positive
benchmark $w^{\mathrm{target}}\in\operatorname{int}\Delta$ has a unique ability
vector $\theta^0$, modulo an additive constant, with
$W_{C_0}(\theta^0) = w^{\mathrm{target}}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$G_{C_0}$ is convex (\Cref{thm:objective}) and, for $C_0\succ0$, $C^\infty$ with
Hessian $\nabla^2 G_{C_0} = \nabla_\theta W_{C_0}$, the winning-probability
Jacobian, which is positive definite on the tangent space
$\{v:\mathbf 1^\top v = 0\}$ and annihilates $\mathbf 1$ (since
$G_{C_0}(\theta + c\mathbf 1) = G_{C_0}(\theta) + c$). Hence $G_{C_0}$ is strictly
convex on the quotient $\R^n/\!\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}$, so its gradient
$W_{C_0}$ is a diffeomorphism onto the relative interior of its range, which is
$\operatorname{int}\Delta$ as any coordinate can be driven to $0$ or $1$ by sending
abilities to $\mp\infty$.
\end{proof}
\noindent The benchmark must lie in the open simplex: a zero target weight sends its
strength $\theta_i$ to $-\infty$ (equivalently its ability $a_i$ to $+\infty$), so
zero-weight assets are dropped or floored before calibration.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Dependence tilt (Thurstone portfolio)}
\label{alg:tilt}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require target weights $w^{\mathrm{target}}\in\Delta$; reference sampler
         $S_{\mathrm{calib}}$; target sampler $S_{\mathrm{tilt}}$ (Gaussian
         $\mathcal{N}(\,\cdot\,, C_{\mathrm{tilt}})$ by default).
\State $a \gets \textsc{Calibrate}(w^{\mathrm{target}}, S_{\mathrm{calib}})$
       \Comment{abilities s.t.\ the race under $S_{\mathrm{calib}}$ yields $w^{\mathrm{target}}$}
\State Draw $X^{(1)},\dots,X^{(M)} \sim S_{\mathrm{tilt}}(a)$ from \emph{fixed} seeds
       \Comment{Gaussian: $X^{(m)} = a + C_{\mathrm{tilt}}^{1/2} Z^{(m)}$}
\State $T^{(m)} \gets \argminop_k X^{(m)}_k$ (a set when tied), \quad
       $w_i \gets \dfrac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}
       \dfrac{\mathbf{1}[\, i \in T^{(m)} \,]}{|T^{(m)}|}$
       \Comment{win frequency, ties split evenly}
\State \Return $w$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The reference correlation $C_{\mathrm{calib}}$ may come from any family that \textcite{cotton2026inversion}
inverts deterministically. The simplest is the independent reference,
$C_{\mathrm{calib}} = I$. A factor reference has one or a few factors common to all
assets. The market reference is its one-factor case,
$C_{\mathrm{calib}} = \beta\beta^\top + \mathrm{diag}(1-\beta_i^2)$, with $\beta_i$
the correlation of asset $i$ with the benchmark-weighted portfolio. A grouped
reference has a factor within each cluster of assets, such as a sector, and a
nested reference adds a common factor across the clusters. A hierarchical
reference is a tree of shared effects, such as market, sector and industry.

Under the independent reference the tilt injects the whole of the estimated
correlation. Under a structured reference the benchmark is reproduced \emph{given}
that structure, and the tilt responds only to the residual correlation in
$C_{\mathrm{tilt}}$. The experiment of \Cref{sec:experiments} uses the one-factor
market reference, and the forecast-combination study of \Cref{sec:combination} the
independent one.

In the Gaussian workhorse a scalar $\phi \in [0,1]$ sets
$C_{\mathrm{tilt}} = (1-\phi)\,C_{\mathrm{calib}} + \phi\,\widehat{C}$. Then
$\phi = 0$ reproduces the benchmark (\Cref{rem:fixedpoint}) and $\phi = 1$ uses the
full estimate $\widehat{C}$.

The construction never inverts a covariance. It needs only a valid correlation for
sampling, repaired if necessary to the nearest correlation matrix, so the
\emph{target} race tolerates ill-conditioned or singular estimates (a singular
$C_{\mathrm{tilt}}$ merely makes some assets effective duplicates, with tie
splitting). The \emph{reference} law is different: the calibration bijection
(\Cref{lem:calib}) and the smooth sensitivity theory (\Cref{thm:smooth}) require a
positive-definite $C_{\mathrm{calib}}$; under a singular reference one works with
limits or selected subgradients. Its feasibility, fixed-point, redundancy,
monotonicity, and objective properties are established in \Cref{sec:properties}.

\section{Computation}
\label{sec:computation}

We mention only the numerical steps not already considered in
\textcite{cotton2026inversion}.

\paragraph{Calibration (deterministic).}
The calibration step, the inverse map from target weights to abilities, is
computed deterministically by the method of \textcite{cotton2026inversion}. A single survival field on a
lattice is shared across all assets, so one pass returns every winning
probability, with no Monte-Carlo error and cost linear in $n$.

For the one-factor reference the assets are conditionally independent given the
market factor, so the same race is evaluated at Gauss--Hermite nodes and
integrated over the factor. The inverse is then a preconditioned fixed-point
iteration on this forward map, warm-started from the independent inverse and run
until the race reproduces the benchmark to $10^{-8}$; the loadings are clipped to
$|\beta_i| \le 0.99$. A zero benchmark weight is floored at $10^{-12}$ before
calibration, and the floored name's ability is then a one-sided bound. This is
the only place quadrature enters.

\paragraph{Tilt (Monte-Carlo).}
The target law is an arbitrary sampler, so the race under it is evaluated by
simulation. A Gaussian target whose covariance has the factor, block, nested or
tree form of \textcite{cotton2026inversion} also admits a deterministic forward race, which we do not use
here.

The \texttt{allocation} package factors the race into a
\emph{draw} (the sampler) and a \emph{scoring} step, the argmin win-frequency
core, so the sampler is a plug-in: the Gaussian default recolours a fixed
standard-normal ensemble by $C_{\mathrm{tilt}}^{1/2}$ (a quasi-Monte-Carlo ensemble may be substituted to sharpen the win frequencies, and often their step-to-step differences, \Cref{sec:smoothness}); a Student-$t$ and a low-rank
factor race (for scale) ship alongside; and any user-supplied callable obeying the
fixed-seed contract supplies an arbitrary law. Tail consistency (\Cref{lem:tail}) follows by
driving the race with a downside-dependent sampler or, within the Gaussian
workhorse, a downside (lower-partial-moment) covariance.

\paragraph{Online updates and scale.}
At rebalancing the correlation estimate moves slowly, and \Cref{sec:smoothness}
shows the weights move smoothly with it. We exploit this by holding the
standard-normal seeds fixed and \emph{transporting} the path ensemble to the new
correlation (recolouring by the updated square root), so turnover tracks genuine
correlation change rather than sampling noise.

For large universes the dense correlation is never formed. A low-rank factor model $C = BB^\top + D$,
$D = \mathrm{diag}(\psi)$, draws $X^{(m)} = a + B Z_f^{(m)} + D^{1/2} Z_\epsilon^{(m)}$
from fixed $k$-dimensional factor seeds and $n$-dimensional idiosyncratic seeds,
so the transport and the argmin are streaming and cost $O(Mnk)$, scaling to
thousands of assets.

A factor basis is identified only up to rotation, and a
rotation between steps would re-colour the factor seeds and register as turnover.
The loadings are therefore taken from a randomized range finder with a fixed
projection, which holds the basis steady except at eigenvalue crossings and sign
flips; aligning each step's loadings to the last by a Procrustes rotation is the
general cure. The covariance estimate itself is supplied by any external online
estimator, so the method interoperates with standard covariance-forecasting
tooling.
